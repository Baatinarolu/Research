HOW SCORING WORKS ▸

Wire searches. A pair like GBP/JPY triggers 4 searches: the pair, pair forecasts, GBP + Bank of England, JPY + Bank of Japan — plus matching stories off the live CNBC & MarketWatch wires.

Pair-aware bias. Headlines are scored for headline direction and currency strength (hawkish/dovish, beats/misses). Strong quote-currency news counts against the pair. The bias meter blends pair flow, base strength and quote strength, weighted by impact, source tier and recency.

Impact score (0–100). Macro keywords (rate decisions, CPI, NFP, earnings…), source tier (Reuters/Bloomberg class = Tier 1) and freshness. Bands: 0–37 low, 38–61 medium, 62–100 high. Every headline's WHY THIS SCORE? box lists the exact matched words and points.

News Conditions Risk (0–100). A separate gauge for how disorderly trading conditions may be: imminent/recent scheduled releases, fresh high-impact bursts, news heat, an already-large daily move and headlines pulling both ways. It is not a direction forecast and not a probability of winning or losing.

Real vs calculated vs demo. Headlines, timestamps, publishers, delayed prices and calendar entries come from live public feeds. Impact, relevance, bias, heat and risk are calculated estimates and can be wrong. Invented data appears only in DEMO mode, under a permanent banner.

Stories. Near-identical headlines from different outlets are clustered into one story — expand “+N more outlets” to see the coverage.

Heat (radar). Last-hour intensity + 24h volume + high-impact count + price move. Always read the story at the publisher before trading on it.

HOW TO READ THIS TERMINAL — NEWS LEVELS, SCORES & TRADING-THROUGH-NEWS RISK

PairWire collects real headlines, then calculates scores to help you decide what to read first and when conditions may be dangerous. Nothing on this page predicts price or tells you to buy or sell.

1. The 30-second reading order

  1. News Conditions Risk + Calendar: is a major release about to land, or just landed?
  2. Desk Brief (plain English): which side of the pair is the news pressure on?
  3. Top Stories: open WHY THIS SCORE?, then read the top story at the publisher.
  4. Chart ◆ markers: did price actually react when the headline landed?
  5. Brokers last: check live spread, margin and stop rules on your own platform — prices here are delayed.

2. News levels: what low / medium / high really mean

LevelTypical examplesHow to treat it
LOW
Impact 0–37		Old commentary, minor data, routine filings, general market colour.Background reading. Rarely a stand-alone reason for a fast move — but verify anything surprising at the source.
MEDIUM
Impact 38–61		PMI/sentiment surveys, central-bank speeches, forecasts, analyst actions, relevant company updates.Can move price if it changes expectations. Read the details, not only the headline.
HIGH
Impact 62–100		Interest-rate decisions, CPI/PCE inflation, jobs/NFP, GDP, major earnings, tariffs, fresh top-wire breaking news.Expect fast, two-way moves around publication. Spread and slippage risk rises. Wait for the actual number/details where possible.
CALENDAR High / Medium / LowForexFactory's expected sensitivity for a scheduled release, shown by the coloured bar in the calendar.This is about the event type, before the result exists. A "High" event that lands exactly as forecast may barely move price.

The market trades the surprise. Reaction mainly depends on Actual vs Forecast, revisions to last month, and details inside the report — not on whether the number looks "good" in isolation. News can also be priced in before release ("buy the rumour, sell the fact").

4. Pair direction, with EUR/USD examples

  • “Euro rises after strong German data” → base (EUR) stronger → pressure up on EUR/USD.
  • “US dollar jumps as rate-cut bets fade” → quote (USD) stronger → pressure down on EUR/USD, even though the dollar headline sounds "bullish".
  • Both currencies strong (or both weak) → the net difference matters, which is why the Bias Meter shows base, quote and pair flow separately.

5. Risks of taking a trade during major news

None of this says "never trade news" — it says know what changes around a release:

  • Spread blow-out: the buy/sell gap can widen many times over for seconds or minutes.
  • Slippage & gaps: market orders and stop-losses can fill well beyond the intended price; a stop is an instruction, not a guaranteed price (unless your broker explicitly guarantees it).
  • Whipsaw: price can spike one way on the first number, then reverse when revisions/details are read.
  • Thin liquidity: fewer resting orders means smaller trades move price further.
  • Broker conditions: margin can rise, execution can slow, and some brokers restrict news trading — rules differ by broker.
  • Headline risk: first flashes can be incomplete or corrected. A score cannot verify a story for you.

Practical checklist: confirm the time in your timezone → know forecast/previous → decide maximum loss before the release → assume worse fill quality than normal → if you do not understand the release, that itself is information.
